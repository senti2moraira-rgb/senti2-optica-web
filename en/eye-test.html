What's included

Everything we check in your eye test

About 30 minutes looking at how you see and how healthy your eyes are.

  • Full prescription check

    We measure your distance and near vision with modern equipment and check whether your glasses or contact lenses are still right for you.

  • Tonometry

    A quick, painless measurement of the pressure inside the eye. It is the key test for catching glaucoma early, especially after 40.

  • Retinal photography

    A digital photo of the back of the eye to assess the retina and optic nerve, kept on file so we can compare year on year.

  • Professional advice

    We explain the results clearly and, if needed, recommend a solution or refer you to an ophthalmologist.

Signs it's time for an eye test

Many eye problems don't hurt and give no warning. If any of these apply to you, book an appointment.

  • Blurry vision

    At a distance (signs, number plates, the TV) or up close (your phone, small print). Or you catch yourself squinting.

  • Screens and tired eyes

    Irritated eyes, headaches at the end of the day or trouble focusing after hours on a computer or phone.

  • Over 40

    You hold your phone or the newspaper further away to read. That's presbyopia, and it has a comfortable fix: varifocals or multifocal contact lenses.

  • More than a year ago

    Even if you see well, adults should be tested every 1 to 2 years, and every year with diabetes or a family history of glaucoma.

Frequently asked questions

Is the eye test really free?

Yes. There is no charge and no obligation to buy. It includes the prescription check, tonometry and retinal photography.

Do I need an appointment?

Yes, so we can give you the time you need. Call +34 966 49 04 40 or WhatsApp +34 722 258 384.

How long does it take?

About 30 minutes. If you wear contact lenses, come with them in and bring your current glasses.

Do you test children?

Yes. We recommend a first test at around age 3 and then once a year during school years.

Who carries out the test?

A qualified, registered optometrist. If we find anything that needs medical attention, we refer you to an ophthalmologist.
